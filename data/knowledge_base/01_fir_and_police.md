# Filing an FIR and Dealing with the Police

## What is an FIR
A First Information Report (FIR) is the written record the police make when they receive information about a **cognizable offence** — a serious offence where the police can arrest without a warrant and start investigation without a court's permission (for example murder, rape, robbery, theft, kidnapping, dowry death, cheating). From 1 July 2024, FIRs are governed by **Section 173 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS)**, which replaced Section 154 of the Code of Criminal Procedure (CrPC).

## How to file an FIR
1. Go to the police station (any station — see Zero FIR below) and tell the officer in charge what happened. The information can be given orally or in writing.
2. If given orally, the officer must write it down and read it back to you. Check it carefully before signing.
3. You are entitled to a **free copy of the FIR** immediately.
4. Note the FIR number, date and the police station.
5. Information can also be given by electronic communication (**e-FIR**). An e-FIR must be signed by the informant within **three days** for it to be taken on record.
6. Information given by a woman about certain sexual offences must be recorded by a woman police officer, and if the woman is temporarily or permanently disabled, at her residence or a place of her choice, with video recording where possible.

## Zero FIR
Under BNSS Section 173(1), an FIR can be registered at **any police station irrespective of where the offence happened**. This is called a "Zero FIR". The police station then transfers it to the station that has jurisdiction. Police cannot refuse to register a complaint saying "this is not our area".

## Preliminary enquiry
For cognizable offences punishable with 3 years or more but less than 7 years, the officer may, with permission of a DSP-rank officer, conduct a preliminary enquiry within **14 days** to see whether a prima facie case exists (BNSS Section 173(3)). For offences punishable with 7 years or more, the FIR must be registered.

## If the police refuse to register your FIR
1. **Send the complaint to the Superintendent of Police (SP)** in writing or by post (BNSS Section 173(4)). The SP may investigate personally or direct an officer to investigate.
2. If that fails, file an application before the **Judicial Magistrate** under **BNSS Section 175(3)** (earlier CrPC 156(3)) asking the court to direct the police to register and investigate. You must first have approached the SP and should attach proof of that.
3. You can also complain to the State Human Rights Commission or file an online complaint on the state police portal.
The Supreme Court in *Lalita Kumari v. Government of Uttar Pradesh (2013)* held that registration of an FIR is mandatory when information discloses a cognizable offence.

## Non-cognizable offences (NC complaint)
For non-cognizable offences (for example simple hurt, some forms of defamation), the police record the information in a daily diary and refer you to the Magistrate. The police cannot investigate without the Magistrate's order.

## Online complaints
Most states let you file complaints or e-FIRs for certain offences (theft of vehicle/mobile, lost documents) on the state police website or the "CCTNS" citizen portal. Cyber crimes can be reported at https://cybercrime.gov.in.
