# Common Offences under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS)

## About the new criminal laws
From **1 July 2024**, three new laws replaced India's colonial-era criminal laws:
- **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (BNS)** replaced the Indian Penal Code, 1860 (IPC).
- **Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS)** replaced the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973 (CrPC).
- **Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 (BSA)** replaced the Indian Evidence Act, 1872.
Offences committed before 1 July 2024 continue to be tried under the IPC; offences on or after that date fall under the BNS.

## New features of the BNS
- **Snatching** is a separate offence (BNS Section 304).
- **Organised crime** (Section 111) and **petty organised crime** (Section 112) are new offences.
- **Terrorist act** is defined within the BNS (Section 113).
- **Mob lynching** — murder by a group of five or more on grounds of race, caste, community, sex, place of birth, language or personal belief is punishable with death or life imprisonment (Section 103(2)).
- **Sexual intercourse by deceitful means**, including a false promise of marriage or employment, is a specific offence (Section 69), punishable up to 10 years.
- **Community service** is introduced as a punishment for some petty offences (for example small-value theft by a first-time offender on return of property, defamation, attempt to commit suicide to restrain a public servant).
- **Sedition (IPC 124A) is removed**; instead Section 152 punishes acts endangering the sovereignty, unity and integrity of India.
- Attempt to commit suicide (IPC 309) is not an offence under the BNS, except where it is done to compel or restrain a public servant (Section 226).

## Cruelty against married women (old 498A)
Cruelty by a husband or his relatives towards a woman — including harassment for dowry and conduct likely to drive her to suicide or cause grave injury — is punishable with imprisonment up to **3 years and fine** (BNS Sections 85 and 86). It is cognizable and non-bailable. Complaints can be made at any police station, the Women Helpline **181**, or to the Protection Officer under the Domestic Violence Act.

## Cheating and fraud
Cheating (BNS Section 318) covers deceiving someone to deliver property or do something they would not otherwise do. Cheating and dishonestly inducing delivery of property is punishable up to **7 years and fine** (Section 318(4), old IPC 420). Online fraud may additionally attract the Information Technology Act, 2000.
