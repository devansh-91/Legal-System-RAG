# Free Legal Aid, Lok Adalats and Helplines

## Who can get free legal aid
Article 39A of the Constitution directs the State to provide free legal aid. Under **Section 12 of the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987**, the following people are entitled to **free legal services** (a free lawyer, court fees, drafting, and advice):
- Women and children.
- Members of Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes.
- Victims of trafficking or begar (forced labour).
- Persons with disabilities.
- Victims of mass disaster, ethnic or caste violence, flood, drought, earthquake or industrial disaster.
- Industrial workmen.
- Persons in custody, including in a protective home, juvenile home or psychiatric hospital.
- Persons whose annual income is below the limit set by the State Government (or ₹5 lakh for cases in the Supreme Court).
- Senior citizens and transgender persons are also covered in many states.

## How to apply
- Visit the **District Legal Services Authority (DLSA)** at the district court complex, or the **Taluka Legal Services Committee** at the taluka court.
- Call the **NALSA helpline 15100**.
- Apply online through the **NALSA Legal Services Management System** at https://nalsa.gov.in.
- Front offices and legal aid clinics exist in many courts, jails, villages and law colleges.
- Under the Legal Aid Defense Counsel System, full-time defence lawyers are available in many districts for accused persons who cannot afford one.

## Lok Adalat
A Lok Adalat ("people's court") settles disputes by **compromise**. Cases pending in court or at the pre-litigation stage can be referred — for example motor accident claims, bank recovery, cheque bounce, family disputes (except divorce), electricity and water bills, and compoundable criminal cases.
- **No court fee** is charged; if a pending case is settled, court fees paid earlier are refunded.
- The award is **final and binding** like a civil court decree, and **no appeal** lies against it (Section 21).
- **National Lok Adalats** are held on fixed dates across the country several times a year.
- **Permanent Lok Adalats** (Section 22B) handle disputes about public utility services (transport, post, telephone, electricity, water, hospitals, insurance, banking) and can decide on merits if settlement fails.

## Finding and engaging a lawyer
Advocates must be enrolled with a State Bar Council. You can ask for the advocate's enrolment number. The Bar Council of India rules prohibit advocates from advertising, so rely on DLSA panels, court bar associations and referrals. Always get a written receipt for fees paid.
