# Domestic Violence, Dowry and Maintenance

## Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005 (PWDVA)
The PWDVA gives **civil remedies** to women facing violence in a domestic relationship — including a wife, a woman in a relationship in the nature of marriage (live-in), mother, sister, daughter or widow living in a shared household.

### What counts as domestic violence
- **Physical abuse** — beating, slapping, pushing, any bodily harm.
- **Sexual abuse** — any conduct of a sexual nature that abuses, humiliates or degrades.
- **Verbal and emotional abuse** — insults, ridicule, name-calling, especially about not having a child or a male child, threats.
- **Economic abuse** — denying money for necessities, stridhan, rent, disposing of her assets, preventing access to the shared household.
- **Dowry-related harassment.**

### Where to go
- **Protection Officer** appointed in every district (contact through the District Women and Child Development office).
- A registered **Service Provider** (NGO).
- The **police** (dial **112**) or the Women Helpline **181**.
- Directly to the **Magistrate** by filing an application under **Section 12** of the Act.

### Reliefs the Magistrate can grant
- **Protection order** (Section 18) — stopping the abuser from committing violence, contacting her or entering her workplace.
- **Residence order** (Section 19) — right to stay in the shared household; she cannot be thrown out, whether or not she has any title to it.
- **Monetary relief** (Section 20) — expenses, loss of earnings, medical costs, maintenance.
- **Custody order** (Section 21) — temporary custody of children.
- **Compensation order** (Section 22) — for injuries including mental torture and emotional distress.
The Magistrate should try to dispose of the case within **60 days** of the first hearing. Breach of a protection order is a criminal offence.

## Dowry
Giving, taking or demanding dowry is an offence under the **Dowry Prohibition Act, 1961** (giving or taking: minimum 5 years' imprisonment and fine of at least ₹15,000 or the value of the dowry, whichever is more). Cruelty for dowry is punishable under BNS Sections 85-86 (old IPC 498A), and **dowry death** within 7 years of marriage under BNS Section 80 (old IPC 304B).

## Maintenance
- **Section 144 BNSS** (old Section 125 CrPC): a wife (including a divorced wife who has not remarried), minor children, adult children unable to maintain themselves due to disability, and parents who cannot maintain themselves can claim monthly maintenance from a person with sufficient means who neglects or refuses to maintain them. This applies to people of **all religions**. Interim maintenance can be ordered during the case, and the application for interim maintenance should be decided within 60 days of notice.
- **Hindu Marriage Act, 1955 Sections 24 and 25** — maintenance pending proceedings and permanent alimony.
- **Hindu Adoptions and Maintenance Act, 1956 Section 18** — a Hindu wife's right to maintenance.
- Muslim women can claim maintenance under Section 144 BNSS as well (*Mohd. Abdul Samad v. State of Telangana*, 2024).

## Divorce basics
- **Mutual consent divorce** (Hindu Marriage Act Section 13B; Special Marriage Act Section 28): the couple must have lived separately for at least **one year**, then file a joint petition. There is normally a **6-month cooling-off period** before the second motion, which the Family Court can waive in suitable cases (*Amardeep Singh v. Harveen Kaur*, 2017). The Supreme Court can also dissolve a marriage for irretrievable breakdown under Article 142 (*Shilpa Sailesh v. Varun Sreenivasan*, 2023).
- **Contested divorce** (Section 13 HMA): grounds include cruelty, adultery, desertion for 2 years, conversion, unsound mind, and not being heard of as alive for 7 years.
- **Triple talaq** (instant talaq-e-biddat) is void and a criminal offence punishable with up to 3 years' imprisonment under the Muslim Women (Protection of Rights on Marriage) Act, 2019.
- Divorce cases are heard by the **Family Court** of the district.
