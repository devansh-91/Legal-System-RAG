# Tenancy, Rent Agreements and Property Basics

## Rent agreements
- A lease of immovable property for **more than 11 months** (from year to year, or any term exceeding one year, or reserving a yearly rent) must be **registered** under **Section 17 of the Registration Act, 1908** and Section 107 of the Transfer of Property Act. This is why many rent agreements are made for 11 months.
- Stamp duty on rent agreements is fixed by each state. Many states (for example Maharashtra) allow online e-registration of leave-and-licence agreements.
- An unregistered agreement that needed registration cannot be used as evidence of the lease terms, though it may be used for collateral purposes.

## Rent control laws
Tenancy is a **state subject**. Each state has its own Rent Control Act (for example the Delhi Rent Control Act, 1958; Maharashtra Rent Control Act, 1999). These laws regulate eviction grounds, fair rent and protection of tenants. The Centre's **Model Tenancy Act, 2021** is a template that states may adopt; it proposes:
- Security deposit capped at **2 months' rent** for residential and 6 months for non-residential premises.
- A written tenancy agreement intimated to a **Rent Authority**.
- Quick disposal by Rent Courts and Rent Tribunals.
- The landlord must give **24 hours' written notice** before entering the premises.
Check whether your state has adopted it.

## Eviction
A landlord **cannot forcibly evict** a tenant by cutting off electricity or water, changing locks or removing belongings. Eviction must follow the rent agreement's notice period or the procedure under the state Rent Act, usually through a court or Rent Authority. For a month-to-month tenancy without a written term, the Transfer of Property Act (Section 106) requires **15 days' notice**. Cutting off essential supplies can be reported to the police and the Rent Controller.

## Security deposit refund
The landlord should refund the security deposit at the end of tenancy after lawful deductions (unpaid rent, damage beyond normal wear and tear). If not refunded, send a legal notice and then file a civil suit for recovery, or approach the Rent Authority where the Model Tenancy Act applies.

## Inheritance — Hindus, Sikhs, Jains, Buddhists
- Under the **Hindu Succession Act, 1956** (as amended in 2005), **daughters are coparceners** in ancestral (joint family) property **with the same rights as sons**, by birth. The Supreme Court in *Vineeta Sharma v. Rakesh Sharma (2020)* held this applies even if the father died before 2005.
- If a Hindu male dies without a will, his property goes first to Class I heirs — including his widow, sons, daughters and mother — in equal shares.

## Wills
- Anyone of sound mind who is an adult can make a will.
- A will must be in writing, signed by the testator, and attested by **two witnesses** (Section 63, Indian Succession Act, 1925).
- **Registration of a will is optional**, but helps prove it. A will can be changed or revoked any time.
- Muslims can bequeath by will only up to **one-third** of their property without heirs' consent.

## Property purchase checks
Before buying property, verify title deeds for at least 30 years, the encumbrance certificate, mutation records, approved building plan, and for new projects the **RERA registration** of the project on the state RERA website. Complaints against builders for delayed possession can be filed before the **State RERA Authority** under the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016, or before a Consumer Commission.
