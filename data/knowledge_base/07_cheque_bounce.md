# Cheque Bounce Cases (Section 138, Negotiable Instruments Act)

## When is cheque bounce an offence
Under **Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881**, dishonour of a cheque is a criminal offence when:
1. The cheque was issued to discharge a **legally enforceable debt or liability** (not a gift or donation).
2. It was presented to the bank **within its validity period** (currently **3 months** from the date on the cheque).
3. It was returned unpaid for **insufficient funds** or because it exceeds the arrangement with the bank (courts also include "account closed", "payment stopped by drawer" and "signature mismatch" in many cases).
4. The payee sent a **written demand notice** to the drawer within **30 days** of receiving the bank's return memo.
5. The drawer **failed to pay within 15 days** of receiving the notice.

## Step-by-step
1. Keep the original cheque and the bank's **return memo** (cheque return slip).
2. Send a **legal demand notice** by registered post/speed post (and email/WhatsApp if possible) within 30 days of the return memo.
3. Wait **15 days** from the date the notice is received.
4. If unpaid, file a **criminal complaint** before the Judicial Magistrate/Metropolitan Magistrate **within 1 month** after the 15-day period ends (Section 142). Delay can be condoned for sufficient cause.
5. The complaint is filed where the payee's bank branch (where the cheque was deposited for collection) is located (Section 142(2)).

## Punishment
Imprisonment up to **2 years**, or a fine up to **twice the cheque amount**, or both. The offence is compoundable — the parties can settle at any stage.

## Presumption and interim compensation
- The court **presumes** the cheque was issued for a debt or liability (Section 139); the accused must rebut this.
- The court may order the drawer to pay **interim compensation up to 20%** of the cheque amount during trial (Section 143A).
- In an appeal against conviction, the appellate court may require deposit of at least **20%** of the fine or compensation (Section 148).

## Civil recovery
Along with the criminal case, the payee may also file a civil suit for recovery of money, including a summary suit under **Order XXXVII of the Code of Civil Procedure**.
