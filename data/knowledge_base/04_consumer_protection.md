# Consumer Complaints under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019

## Who is a consumer
A consumer is any person who buys goods or hires/avails services for consideration, **including online purchases, teleshopping and multi-level marketing**. A person who buys goods for resale or for a commercial purpose is generally not a "consumer" (except goods used exclusively to earn a livelihood by self-employment).

## What you can complain about
- Defective goods or deficient services (for example a faulty phone, poor bank service, delayed flat possession by a builder, insurance claim wrongly rejected, medical negligence).
- **Unfair trade practices** and misleading advertisements.
- Overcharging above the MRP or the displayed price.
- Hazardous goods or services.
- Unfair contract terms.

## First step: try to resolve
1. Send a written complaint/legal notice to the seller or service provider with bill copies, and keep proof.
2. Call the **National Consumer Helpline 1915** or use the NCH app / https://consumerhelpline.gov.in — it forwards grievances to companies for pre-litigation settlement.

## Where to file (pecuniary jurisdiction)
Jurisdiction depends on the **value of goods or services paid (consideration)**, not the compensation claimed (2021 Rules):
- **District Consumer Commission** — up to **₹50 lakh**.
- **State Consumer Commission** — above ₹50 lakh and up to **₹2 crore**.
- **National Consumer Commission (NCDRC)** — above **₹2 crore**.

A complaint can be filed where **the complainant resides or works for gain**, not only where the opposite party is located (Section 34).

## How to file
- Online through the **e-Daakhil / e-Jagriti** portal (https://e-jagriti.gov.in), or physically at the Commission.
- No lawyer is required; you can argue your own case.
- Attach bills, receipts, warranty cards, correspondence and a short affidavit.
- Court fees are nominal. **No fee is payable for claims up to ₹5 lakh.**

## Time limit
A complaint must be filed within **2 years** from the date the cause of action arose (Section 69). A delay can be condoned if you show sufficient cause.

## Reliefs available
The Commission can order repair or replacement, refund, compensation for loss or injury, removal of defects, discontinuance of unfair trade practices, punitive damages and litigation costs.

## Appeals
- District Commission order → appeal to State Commission within **45 days**.
- State Commission order → appeal to National Commission within **30 days**.
- National Commission order → appeal to the Supreme Court within **30 days**.

## E-commerce
The Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020 require online platforms to display seller details, return/refund policies, and to have a grievance officer who must acknowledge complaints within 48 hours and resolve them within one month.
