# Workplace Rights — Wages, Gratuity and Sexual Harassment (POSH)

## Sexual harassment at workplace (POSH Act, 2013)
- Every employer with **10 or more employees** must constitute an **Internal Committee (IC)**, headed by a senior woman employee and including an external member from an NGO or person familiar with the issue.
- Where an establishment has fewer than 10 employees, or the complaint is against the employer, the complaint goes to the **Local Committee** set up by the District Officer.
- A complaint must be made in writing within **3 months** of the incident (or of the last incident, if a series). This can be extended by another 3 months for valid reasons.
- The IC must complete its inquiry within **90 days**. The employer must act on the report within **60 days**.
- Sexual harassment includes unwelcome physical contact, demand for sexual favours, sexually coloured remarks, showing pornography and any other unwelcome physical, verbal or non-verbal conduct of a sexual nature, including implied promises of preferential treatment or threats about employment.
- The woman may also file a criminal complaint (for example BNS Section 75 — sexual harassment).
- Complaints can also be filed on the government's **SHe-Box** portal.

## Gratuity
- Payable to an employee who has rendered **at least 5 years of continuous service** on resignation, retirement, death or disablement (5 years is not required in case of death or disablement).
- Formula (for establishments covered by the Act): **15 days' last drawn wages (basic + DA) × completed years of service**, where monthly wage ÷ 26 gives the daily wage.
- The maximum gratuity under the Act is **₹20 lakh**.
- The employer must pay within **30 days** of it becoming payable; otherwise simple interest is payable. Disputes go to the **Controlling Authority** under the Act (usually the Assistant Labour Commissioner).
- Under the Code on Social Security, 2020, **fixed-term employees** become eligible for gratuity after **one year** of service.

## Labour Codes
Parliament consolidated 29 central labour laws into four Labour Codes: the **Code on Wages, 2019**, the **Industrial Relations Code, 2020**, the **Code on Social Security, 2020** and the **Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020**. The Central Government brought the Codes into force in **November 2025**; many detailed rules are framed by states, so check your state's notified rules. Key points include a universal right to minimum wages, timely payment of wages, equal remuneration regardless of gender, and social security coverage extended to gig and platform workers.

## Unpaid salary
1. Send a written demand/legal notice to the employer.
2. File a complaint with the **Labour Commissioner / Labour Inspector** of your area (many states accept online complaints; the central **Samadhan** portal handles industrial disputes).
3. Workmen can raise an industrial dispute; others may file a civil suit for recovery. Cheques given for salary that bounce attract Section 138 of the NI Act.

## Maternity benefit
Under the **Maternity Benefit Act, 1961** (amended 2017), a woman who has worked at least **80 days** in the 12 months before expected delivery is entitled to **26 weeks** of paid maternity leave for the first two children (12 weeks from the third child). Adoptive and commissioning mothers of a child under 3 months get 12 weeks. Establishments with 50 or more employees must provide a **crèche** facility. Dismissal during maternity leave is prohibited.

## Provident Fund
Employees in establishments covered by the EPF Act contribute **12%** of basic wages + DA; the employer contributes an equal amount. You can check your balance and file grievances on the EPFO portal (https://epfigms.gov.in) or UMANG app.
