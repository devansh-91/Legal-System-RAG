# Rights on Arrest and Bail

## Rights of an arrested person
Every arrested person in India has the following rights under Article 22 of the Constitution and the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS):
- **Right to know the grounds of arrest** and, for bailable offences, that they have a right to bail (BNSS Section 47).
- **Right to inform a relative or friend** about the arrest and the place of detention (BNSS Section 48). The police must inform them.
- **Right to meet an advocate** of choice during interrogation, though not throughout (BNSS Section 38).
- **Right to be produced before a Magistrate within 24 hours** of arrest, excluding travel time (BNSS Section 58; Article 22(2)). Detention beyond this without a Magistrate's order is illegal.
- **Right to medical examination** (BNSS Section 53).
- **Right to free legal aid** if you cannot afford a lawyer (Article 39A; Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987).
- **Right against self-incrimination** — no person accused of an offence can be compelled to be a witness against themselves (Article 20(3)).
- The arresting officer must wear a clear name tag, prepare an **arrest memo** attested by a witness (family member or respectable local person) and signed by the arrested person (as per *D.K. Basu v. State of West Bengal*, 1997, now reflected in BNSS Section 36).

## Arrest of women
A woman should ordinarily be arrested only by a woman police officer. **No woman shall be arrested after sunset and before sunrise**, except in exceptional circumstances with the prior permission of the Judicial Magistrate, obtained through a woman police officer (BNSS Section 43(5)).

## Notice of appearance instead of arrest
For offences punishable up to 7 years, the police need not arrest; they may issue a **notice of appearance** (BNSS Section 35(3)). If the person complies with the notice, they should not be arrested unless the officer records reasons. The Supreme Court in *Arnesh Kumar v. State of Bihar (2014)* warned against routine arrests in such cases.

## Types of bail
- **Bailable offences** — bail is a right. The police officer or court must release the person on bail (BNSS Section 478).
- **Non-bailable offences** — bail is at the court's discretion (BNSS Section 480). Courts consider nature of the offence, evidence, risk of absconding and tampering with witnesses.
- **Anticipatory bail** — a person who fears arrest for a non-bailable offence can apply to the **Sessions Court or High Court** for a direction that they be released on bail if arrested (BNSS Section 482; earlier CrPC Section 438).
- **Default (statutory) bail** — if the police do not file the charge-sheet within **60 days** (offences punishable up to 10 years) or **90 days** (offences punishable with death, life imprisonment or 10+ years), the accused becomes entitled to bail if they apply (BNSS Section 187).

## Undertrial prisoners
Under BNSS Section 479, an undertrial who has spent **half of the maximum sentence** for the offence in custody must be released on bail. A **first-time offender** must be released on bail after spending **one-third** of the maximum sentence. This does not apply to offences punishable with death or life imprisonment.
