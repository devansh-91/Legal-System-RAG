# Rights of Senior Citizens and Children

## Senior citizens and parents
Under the **Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act, 2007**:
- Parents (including grandparents and step-parents) and senior citizens (60 years and above) who cannot maintain themselves can claim **monthly maintenance** from their children or grandchildren; a childless senior citizen can claim from a relative who will inherit their property.
- Apply to the **Maintenance Tribunal** (usually the Sub-Divisional Magistrate/SDM). The Tribunal can order maintenance of up to **₹10,000 per month** and should decide within **90 days**.
- If a senior citizen transfers property by gift or otherwise on the condition that the transferee will provide basic amenities, and the transferee fails to do so, the **transfer can be declared void** by the Tribunal (Section 23).
- Abandoning a senior citizen is punishable with up to 3 months' imprisonment or a fine of up to ₹5,000, or both (Section 24).
- Helpline: **Elderline 14567**.

## Protection of children from sexual offences (POCSO Act, 2012)
- Applies to all persons below **18 years**, regardless of gender.
- **Reporting is mandatory**: anyone who knows of such an offence must report it to the police or the Special Juvenile Police Unit; failure to report is punishable (Section 21).
- The child's statement should be recorded at the child's home or a place of their choice, preferably by a woman police officer not in uniform, and the child's identity must never be disclosed in media.
- Cases are tried by **Special Courts**, and trials should be completed within one year.
- Call **CHILDLINE 1098** or **112** in an emergency. Complaints can also be filed on the NCPCR POCSO e-box.

## Child marriage
Under the **Prohibition of Child Marriage Act, 2006**, a marriage where the girl is below **18** or the boy is below **21** is a child marriage. It is **voidable** at the option of the party who was a child (petition can be filed up to two years after attaining majority). Performing, conducting or promoting a child marriage is punishable with up to 2 years' rigorous imprisonment and a fine of up to ₹1 lakh. Report to the **Child Marriage Prohibition Officer**, police, or 1098.

## Child labour
Employment of children below **14 years** in any occupation or process is prohibited, except helping family enterprises after school hours and as child artists under conditions. Adolescents aged **14–18** cannot be employed in hazardous occupations. Violations are punishable with imprisonment of 6 months to 2 years and/or a fine of ₹20,000 to ₹50,000. Report via the **PENCIL portal** or 1098.

## Right to education
Children aged 6 to 14 have a right to free and compulsory education in a neighbourhood school (Article 21A; RTE Act, 2009). Private unaided schools must reserve **25%** of entry-level seats for children from economically weaker and disadvantaged groups. Schools cannot charge capitation fees or subject children to screening, physical punishment or mental harassment.

## Juveniles in conflict with law
Children below 18 accused of offences are dealt with by the **Juvenile Justice Board**, not regular criminal courts, under the Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2015. A child aged 16–18 accused of a heinous offence (minimum punishment of 7 years or more) may, after a preliminary assessment, be tried as an adult by the Children's Court.
