# Fundamental Rights and Writ Remedies

## Fundamental Rights (Part III of the Constitution)
- **Article 14** — Equality before law and equal protection of the laws.
- **Article 15** — No discrimination on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex or place of birth. The State may make special provisions for women, children, and socially and educationally backward classes, SCs and STs.
- **Article 16** — Equality of opportunity in public employment.
- **Article 17** — Abolition of untouchability; its practice in any form is an offence (see the Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955 and the SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989).
- **Article 19** — Six freedoms for citizens: speech and expression; peaceful assembly without arms; forming associations or unions; moving freely throughout India; residing and settling anywhere in India; practising any profession, occupation, trade or business — subject to reasonable restrictions.
- **Article 20** — Protection in respect of conviction: no retrospective criminal law, no double jeopardy, no compulsion to be a witness against oneself.
- **Article 21** — Protection of life and personal liberty. The Supreme Court has read into it the rights to live with dignity, livelihood, shelter, health, clean environment, speedy trial, free legal aid, **privacy** (*Justice K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India*, 2017) and more.
- **Article 21A** — Free and compulsory education for children aged **6 to 14 years** (see the Right to Education Act, 2009, which also reserves 25% seats in private unaided schools for children from weaker sections).
- **Article 22** — Protection against arbitrary arrest and detention (grounds of arrest, lawyer, production before magistrate within 24 hours).
- **Articles 23-24** — Prohibition of human trafficking and forced labour; no child below 14 may be employed in factories, mines or hazardous employment.
- **Articles 25-28** — Freedom of conscience and religion.
- **Articles 29-30** — Cultural and educational rights of minorities.
- **Article 32** — Right to approach the **Supreme Court** directly to enforce fundamental rights.

Fundamental rights are mainly enforceable against the "State" (Article 12), which includes government bodies and authorities; some (such as Articles 17, 23 and 24) apply against private persons too.

## Writs
The Supreme Court (Article 32) and **High Courts (Article 226)** can issue five kinds of writs. High Courts can issue writs for fundamental rights **and for any other legal right**, so they are usually the first forum.
- **Habeas corpus** — "produce the body": to free a person illegally detained.
- **Mandamus** — orders a public authority to perform a legal duty it has failed to perform.
- **Prohibition** — stops a lower court or tribunal from exceeding its jurisdiction.
- **Certiorari** — quashes an order passed by a lower court or tribunal without jurisdiction or in violation of natural justice.
- **Quo warranto** — questions the legal right of a person holding a public office.

## Public Interest Litigation (PIL)
Any public-spirited person can file a PIL in the High Court or Supreme Court on behalf of those who cannot approach the court themselves (for example prisoners, bonded labourers, environmental issues). Courts have even treated letters and postcards as PILs.
