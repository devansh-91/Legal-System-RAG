# Cyber Crime, Online Fraud and Data Protection

## What to do immediately after online financial fraud
1. **Call 1930** (National Cyber Crime Helpline) immediately. Reporting within the first hours ("golden hour") greatly improves the chances of freezing the money in the fraudster's account.
2. Report on **https://cybercrime.gov.in** ("Report other cyber crimes" / financial fraud) and note the acknowledgement number.
3. Inform your **bank** and ask them to block your card/UPI and raise a dispute. Under RBI rules on customer liability, if you report an unauthorised electronic transaction within **3 working days** and the fraud was not due to your negligence, your liability is **zero**; the bank must credit the amount (shadow reversal) within **10 working days**.
4. Keep screenshots, transaction IDs, phone numbers and chat records.
5. Visit the local police station or cyber cell to file an FIR if needed.

## "Digital arrest" scams
Government agencies such as the CBI, police, ED, customs or RBI **never arrest anyone over a video call** and never ask you to transfer money to "verify" your account. There is no such thing as a "digital arrest" in Indian law. Disconnect, do not pay, and report to 1930 / cybercrime.gov.in.

## Key offences under the IT Act, 2000
- **Section 66C** — identity theft (fraudulent use of someone's password, electronic signature or unique identification): up to 3 years and fine up to ₹1 lakh.
- **Section 66D** — cheating by personation using a computer resource (fake profiles, fake customer-care calls): up to 3 years and fine up to ₹1 lakh.
- **Section 66E** — violation of privacy by capturing or publishing private images: up to 3 years or fine up to ₹2 lakh.
- **Section 67** — publishing or transmitting obscene material electronically; **Section 67A** — sexually explicit material; **Section 67B** — child sexual abuse material (strictly punishable).
- **Section 43 read with 66** — hacking, data theft, introducing viruses.
Offences such as cheating (BNS 318), extortion (BNS 308), stalking including online stalking (BNS 78) and defamation (BNS 356) also apply to online conduct.

## Harassment on social media
- Report the account/content on the platform. Under the IT (Intermediary Guidelines) Rules, 2021, platforms must have a **Grievance Officer** and remove non-consensual intimate images within **24 hours** of a complaint.
- Women and children can report sexual content on cybercrime.gov.in, including anonymously.

## Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023
The DPDP Act gives individuals ("data principals") the right to information about how their personal data is processed, the right to correction and erasure, and the right to grievance redressal. Organisations ("data fiduciaries") must obtain clear consent and protect data. Complaints go to the **Data Protection Board of India** after first approaching the organisation.
